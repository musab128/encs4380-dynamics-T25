Module 3: Second-Order Response

Explore an accelerometer-like mass–spring–damper system: change its physical parameters to observe damping, transient metrics, poles, and state-space trajectories.

Mass–spring–damper controls

m ẍ + b ẋ + kx = F(t)
ωn = √(k/m) ; ζ = b/(2√(km))

Preset buttons use m = 1 and k = 4; they set b to 2, 4, or 8. Input is a unit step force F(t) = 1 and initial displacement/velocity are zero.

Step response & transient measurements

Rise time is measured from 10% to 90% of the final value (first upward crossings). Settling time uses a ±2% band. The graph uses the exact analytical response, with numerical sampling for displayed measurements.

Poles in the s-plane

Characteristic equation: s² + (b/m)s + k/m = 0.

State-space representation

x₁ = displacement; x₂ = velocity; u = F(t), y = [1 0]x.

Eigenvalues of A are exactly the system poles.

Phase plane: velocity vs displacement

State trajectory [x₁(t), x₂(t)] during the unit-step response. Underdamping spirals toward the equilibrium (1/k, 0).
